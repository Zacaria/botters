## Direction

Target roughly 60 seconds, 16:9, 1080p, using real rendered gameplay, purposeful cuts, readable short captions and a musical rise with a contrast/breath before the climax. These are project choices, not proven universal conversion optima. Start with the game and its distinctive strategic agency, not a long studio logo. End with one clear action and the real Botters identity; do not invent a Steam listing, wishlist page, launch date or testimonial.

The viewer must understand: capture planets to grow, confront the opposing swarm, turn strategy into ordered gambits, and intervene directly when needed. The appeal is seeing a strategy become observable behavior, then changing it as the battle develops—not merely watching dots move. Show each claim through an actual consequence. AI writes/configures gambits; the footage must not imply continuous model inference, a model-controlled house opponent or a supported client journey that was not actually executed.

Initial narrative copy is French for the owner's first review; keep the shot/edit script separable so an English version can reuse the same footage. Music plus meaningful on-screen captions is the default; narration is optional only if it materially improves this cut. The story must remain understandable when muted.

## Five-beat first-cut storyboard

1. **0–8 s — Hook.** A readable actual gambit immediately followed by its decisive consequence in a moving confrontation. Caption: « Vos règles. Votre essaim. » Establish the strategic agency, game, colors and stakes within the opening; no static opening lecture.
2. **8–22 s — Goal.** A real planet capture, particle growth and pressure from the opponent. Caption: « Capturez des planètes. Faites grandir votre essaim. » Preserve spatial clarity across cuts.
3. **22–38 s — Distinctive agency.** Show a readable real gambit, then its observed effect in the same recorded match. Caption: « Définissez les règles. Regardez-les agir. » Use only relevant HUD details, framed/enlarged rather than an unreadable full interface. No fabricated chat or simulated model response.
4. **38–51 s — Tension and adaptation.** An actual threat, meaningful manual movement and release of that same swarm to gambits. Caption: « Reprenez la main au bon moment. » The order must cause visible travel, not repeat the old near-zero-distance R1 shot. Build music and edit around actual action beats, with one short contrast before the climax.
5. **51–60 s — Payoff and invitation.** A real outcome or dramatic capture, then BOTTERS and « Découvrez Botters. » Do not fake a completed victory if it was not captured. Hold the final invitation long enough to read; retain necessary music credits.

These are editable production targets, not instructions to manufacture game state or force a claim unsupported by the captured footage. An awkward shot should be recaptured or rewritten, not explained away in its transcript.

## Source-backed production choices

- Valve / Steamworks, **Trailers**: prioritize actual gameplay and the player's perspective in the first store trailer. HUD can be useful. Steam-style does not mean inventing a cinematic that misrepresents the product. https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/trailer
- Derek Lieu, **Game Trailer Structure — Genre, Hook, Content** (2021): establish what kind of game it is and why it is distinctive before enumerating content. https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2021/4/12/game-trailer-structure-genre-hook-content
- Derek Lieu, **How to Hook the Audience and How Quickly to Do It** (2022): introduce the differentiating mechanic early rather than hiding it behind familiar features. https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2022/10/24/how-to-hook-the-audience-and-how-quickly-to-do-it
- Derek Lieu, **What Makes a Successful and Memorable Game Trailer**, GDC: teach, excite and tease; sequence shots for cause/effect, vary intensity and use music intentionally. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QWd7F0z1W_Y
- Derek Lieu, **Hades II Early Access Trailer Review** (2024): readable text supports muted Steam viewing; do not copy the long opening or update-trailer structure of an established sequel for an unknown game. https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2024/5/19/hades-ii-early-access-trailer-review

## Lesson

When the owner asks for an announcement trailer, treat narrative, goal, distinctive appeal, music, readable captions and editing as acceptance criteria. A technically valid gameplay capture is footage, not a finished promotional trailer.
